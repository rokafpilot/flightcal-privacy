FlightCal User Guide

App version 1.0.21 · build 30
Service provider: SmartPilots
Updated: November 9, 2026

FlightCal is a pocket flight computer for pilots. It reproduces the classic E6-B style calculator together with unit conversion, currency conversion, a scientific calculator, holdover time, airport weather, a global weather map, a dictionary and a stopwatch — all driven by the same physical keypad on every screen.

1. Getting Started

Every screen shares the same layout: the menu name at the top left, the current UTC clock at the top right, a list of value lines in the middle, and the keypad at the bottom. The active line is highlighted, and results are shown in green.

The trial banner under the header shows the 30-day trial period and its end date, for example 30 DAY TRIAL · ENDS 2026.12.09, with TAP TO UNLOCK underneath to purchase the full version.

Entering a value

  • Move the cursor by swiping vertically on the LCD (a swipe of 36 px or more), or by tapping the line directly.
  • Start editing by tapping the line or pressing =; type digits to enter a value.
  • C clears the current entry, BKSP deletes one character.
  • +− changes the sign, . enters a decimal point.

Time lines accept a colon, but you can also just type digits. Digits are read from the right: four digits are read as HHMM and two digits as minutes. So typing 130 gives 01:30, and 0130 gives 01:30 as well.

Menus marked with a check mark keep their last input, so you can leave a screen and come back without retyping values.

  • Swipe vertically on the LCD or tap a line to move the cursor.
  • Confirm / edit with =, or tap the line.
  • BKSP doubles as BACK: it deletes a character while editing, otherwise it returns to the previous screen.

3. E6-B (FLT)

The E6-B menu holds the classic flight computer functions. Select an item with the cursor and open it with =. Each function is a short list of lines: fill the inputs and the result appears in green. When a screen offers several modes, the mode tabs are shown just under the header.

3.1 Density Altitude

Lines: Press Alt, OAT, Dens Alt. Enter pressure altitude and outside air temperature and the density altitude is computed. On the pressure altitude line UNIT/CONV switches ft ↔ m, and on the OAT line it switches °C ↔ °F.

3.2 Distance

Lines: Dist (NM), Dur (HMS), GS (KT). Enter any two values to solve the third — distance, duration or ground speed. The duration line accepts time in hours and minutes.

3.3 Endurance

Lines: Wt (fuel weight) and Rate (fuel flow), with the endurance time as the result. The endurance mode is one of the three modes of the fuel screen, so you can switch to Fuel Burn or Fuel Rate without going back to the menu.

3.4 Fuel Consumption

This is the Fuel Rate mode of the same screen. Enter fuel weight and duration and the consumption rate is returned in LB/HR, or KG/HR when the weight unit is set to kilograms in Settings.

3.5 Pressure Altitude

Lines: Fld Elev, Altim, Press Alt. The altimeter setting defaults to 1013.25. UNIT/CONV switches the altimeter between hPa and inHg, and the elevation between ft and m.

3.6 Rate of Descent

The climb / descent screen has Climb and Descent tabs and the lines Rate, Alt Chg, GS, Fuel, Time, Dist, Burn. Units are FPM, FT, KT, LB·HR, HMS, NM and LBS. Fill the rate, the altitude change and the ground speed, and the time, distance and fuel burned are solved together.

3.7 Rate of Descent Mach

Lines: Mach, Angle (default 3.0), OAT (default 15 °C / 59 °F), RoD, TAS, Spd Sound. Given a Mach number and a descent angle it returns the rate of descent in FPM, the TAS and the local speed of sound in knots. The OAT line switches between °C and °F.

3.8 Reserve Fuel

Lines: Req Fuel, Rsv %, Alt Fuel, Hold Fuel, Conting, Reserve, Total Req. The reserve percentage defaults to 15 %, alternate and holding fuel to 0. Contingency is the trip fuel times the reserve percentage; reserve is contingency plus alternate plus holding fuel; total required is trip fuel plus reserve.

3.9 Speed (Airspeed)

Five modes: Planned TAS, Actual TAS, Required CAS, Planned MACH#, Actual MACH#. Planned TAS takes CAS, OAT and pressure altitude and returns TAS, TAT and Mach. Actual TAS uses TAT instead of OAT and returns OAT. Required CAS reverses the calculation, giving CAS from TAS. The two Mach modes work with Mach and temperature only.

3.10 Time (ETA)

Lines: Dist (NM), GS (KT), Time (HMS). A quick time en route calculation from distance and ground speed, or the ground speed required to cover a distance in a given time.

3.11 Top of Descent

Lines: Alt, Tgt, GS, Path (default 3.0°), Dist, Time, ROD. Enter the current altitude, the target altitude and the ground speed to get the distance and time to start down, plus the required rate of descent.

3.12 Wind Component

Lines: RWY, WDIR, WSPD, HEAD, TAIL, CROSS. Enter the runway direction, wind direction and wind speed and the headwind, tailwind and crosswind components are shown; the crosswind is marked left or right.

3.13 Fuel Uplift

Lines: Tot Req, Current, Density, Uplift, Uplift Gal, Tot Onbd. Density defaults to 6.7 lb/gal. The uplift is the required fuel minus the fuel already on board, converted to gallons at the given density, and the total on board after uplifting is shown as well.

3.14 Cold Temp Correction

Lines: Indicated, Fld Elev, OAT, ΔISA, Corr, Corr Alt. It applies the cold temperature altitude correction to an indicated altitude for a given field elevation and OAT, and displays the ISA deviation and the corrected altitude. Altitudes switch between ft and m, and the temperature between °C and °F.

5. CURR — Currency

The converter screen has five lines. Type the Amount, set SET FROM and CONV TO, and the converted Result appears in green. The ⇄ key between the two currency lines reverses the pair, and the Rate line shows the live quote, for example 1 USD = 1334.5069 KRW.

Press = on a currency line to open the searchable currency list. Selecting the same currency on both sides is not allowed, so the app always keeps a valid pair.

Tap the rate line to open the rate chart; BKSP or C closes it. The status at the end of the rate line tells you where the number came from:

Status Meaning
LIVE A fresh quote fetched less than a minute ago.
12m ago Fetched recently; shown in minutes, then hours and minutes.
OFFLINE · CACHED 1D 3h The network is unavailable, so the last stored rate is used and its age is shown.
DEFAULT · BUILT-IN No stored rate yet; the built-in reference rate is used.

The CURR menu also lists 12 frequently used currency presets, which open the converter already set to that pair:

USD → KRW · EUR → USD · USD → JPY · JPY → KRW · CNY → KRW · SGD → KRW · GBP → USD · HKD → KRW · THB → KRW · AED → USD · CAD → USD · AUD → USD

The preset list is built around a default home currency (KRW by default, changeable in SET → Home Currency).

6. CALC — Calculator

Four modes share one screen and are selected with the mode segment. Pressing CALC cycles through them: STD → SCI → HMS → DUTY → STD.

6.1 STD

A standard four-function calculator with a four-line history above the result. Press = to evaluate.

6.2 SCI

Scientific mode relabels the function keys. DUTY = sin, HOT = cos, CURR = ln, FLT = log, CONV = x², MAP = 1/x, STOP = π and DICT = eˣ. SET becomes INV to enable the inverse functions. Functions without a dedicated key (xʸ, ʸ√x, n!, tan) are buttons inside the display.

6.3 HMS

A dedicated time calculator. Enter hours, minutes and seconds with the colon key and multiply or divide by a plain number; the result stays in hours, minutes and seconds.

6.4 DUTY

The Duty mode is the flight and duty time calculator. Choose the crew complement and enter the times; the app applies the duty-time limits and shows the result.

7. HOT — Holdover Time

Lines: Time of Day, Weather, OAT (Outside), Visibility, Fluid, Mixture Ratio, Holdover HOT.

  • Time of Day switches between DAY (06:00–19:00) and NIGHT (19:00–06:00) with the arrow keys or a tap.
  • Weather, Visibility, Fluid and Mixture Ratio open their full list with = or a tap.
  • OAT is typed in directly, in whole degrees, and follows the temperature unit in Settings. The UNIT/CONV key toggles °C ↔ °F.

The weather list offers three AUTO families (derived from visibility, time of day and temperature) and 16 METAR reported conditions. The result line shows the holdover time in minutes. If the OAT is above freezing the screen reports that de-icing is not needed; if the temperature is below the fluid's operating range, the result shows as unavailable instead of a time.

Press = on the result line, or tap it, to start the HOT Timer already loaded with the calculated minutes. The timer counts down and beeps three times when it reaches zero.

8. AIRPORT

AIRPORT (the DUTY key) opens the airport page. Search by ICAO or IATA code with the on-screen keypad. The BACK/HOME button steps back through the airports you opened, ★ SAVE stores the airport (up to 4 saved airports) and REFRESH reloads the data with a loading indicator.

Two tabs are available: WX · METAR / TAF and NOTAM.

8.2 NOTAM tab

  • The header shows how many notices were loaded, with counts for CRITICAL and WARN.
  • DECODE expands the raw NOTAM text into plain descriptions.
  • Filters: ALL, CRITICAL, WARN and INFO.
  • If nothing can be loaded, NOTAM UNAVAILABLE · RETRY is shown.

Recent results are cached, so the last weather and NOTAM you looked at is still readable without a connection.

10. DICT — Dictionary

The dictionary is a fully offline set of 5,268 ICAO, NOTAM, weather and AIP terms for pilots and controllers. Type any part of a term to search; the list filters as you type. Use the cursor to browse the full A–Z list page by page.

Tag filters cover NOTAM, WEATHER, AIP and ICAO. The number pad doubles as a T9 letter pad (2 = ABC, 3 = DEF …) and SET becomes ABC to return to the numeric layout. Press = on an entry to read the full definition and BKSP to return to the list.

12. STOP / TIMER

The timer screen has two tools and toggles between them: STOPWATCH and COUNTDOWN.

  • C starts and pauses.
  • BKSP resets to zero.
  • In countdown mode wind the dial to set the time (one revolution = 60 minutes); the maximum is 99:59:59.

The screen shows an analog chronograph face together with a digital readout, so it can be read at a glance in the cockpit. The timer beeps three times at 880 Hz when the countdown reaches zero.

13. Data, Caching and Trial

  • Settings, last inputs and cached weather, NOTAM and exchange rates are stored on the device, so the app keeps working without a connection where possible.
  • Weather, NOTAM and exchange-rate features need a network connection to refresh; when offline, the last cached values are shown and marked accordingly.
  • FlightCal runs as a 30-day trial, after which the full version can be unlocked through the App Store or Google Play. Purchase handling is described in the Privacy Policy.
